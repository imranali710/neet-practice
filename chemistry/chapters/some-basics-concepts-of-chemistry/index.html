Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry

Mole concept, stoichiometry, limiting reagent, and concentration units.

Essential Formula Cards

Number of Moles (n)

n = w / M = N / N_A = V / 22.4 L (STP)

w = mass in grams, M = molar mass, N = number of particles, N_A = 6.022 × 10²³.

Molarity (M)

M = (w_B × 1000) / (M_B × V_sol in mL)

Temperature dependent. Number of moles of solute dissolved per litre of solution.

Molality (m)

m = (w_B × 1000) / (M_B × W_solvent in g)

Temperature independent. Number of moles of solute per kg of solvent.

Mole Fraction (X)

X_A = n_A / (n_A + n_B) ; X_A + X_B = 1

Unitless and temperature independent ratio of moles of a component to total moles.

Empirical to Molecular Formula

Molecular Formula = n × (Empirical Formula)

n = (Molar mass) / (Empirical formula mass).

Dilution Formula

M1 × V1 = M2 × V2

For mixing solutions of the same solute: M_mix = (M1V1 + M2V2) / (V1 + V2).

22.7 L (Under older convention at 1 atm it was 22.4 L).
Which concentration terms are independent of temperature? Click to flip
Molality (m), Mole fraction (X), and Mass percentage (% w/w) (because mass does not depend on temperature).
How do you find the Limiting Reagent in a reaction? Click to flip
Divide given moles of each reactant by its stoichiometric coefficient in the balanced equation: (Moles / Stoichiometric Coefficient). The smallest value represents the limiting reagent.
